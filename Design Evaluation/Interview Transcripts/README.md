## Main findings

### What worked

- All five participants completed the planned core workflow across the wall whiteboard and table paper.
- The wall whiteboard was the easiest surface to understand and use.
- Participants described the concept as useful for students, especially students who are easily distracted during online classes. The classroom feeling was also seen as useful for homeschooling.
- Participants understood that the whiteboard could be used for shared teaching while the table paper could be used for personal notes.
- Confidence was high in the recorded sessions: P1 rated the prototype 4 out of 5, while P2 and P3 rated it 5 out of 5. P4 and P5 were marked satisfied on the checklists.

### What needs improvement

- The table paper tools were not immediately easy to read or find. Participants often needed to move closer or needed a prompt before noticing Pencil, Eraser, and Clear Paper.
- P1 reported that paper drawing felt slightly glitchy, even though the core paper task was completed. This remains a retest item rather than a closed issue.
- The teleport control was not immediately noticed by P1. P2 and P3 found movement more clearly, so the visibility of the navigation control is inconsistent.
- P3 found it difficult to look up and down naturally. Future versions should provide clearer vertical view control or a more comfortable camera interaction.
- Future suggestions included a larger classroom, more tables, more whiteboard drawing features, and possible multiplayer support.

## Testing method

The sessions used a short moderated task test with think aloud feedback. Participants were asked to find the two surfaces, draw a horizontal line, vertical line, and circle, change colour, erase, clear the board, move to a table, use the paper tools, and clear the paper. P1, P2, and P3 gave permission for audio recording and have full transcript PDFs. P4 and P5 were documented with checklists only, so there are no invented quotes or full transcripts for those sessions.

## Evidence boundary

The transcripts show participant behaviour and comments from three recorded sessions. The P4 and P5 records confirm task completion and satisfaction only. The findings should therefore be used to guide the next prototype and further testing, not treated as a complete study of all XR classroom users.
