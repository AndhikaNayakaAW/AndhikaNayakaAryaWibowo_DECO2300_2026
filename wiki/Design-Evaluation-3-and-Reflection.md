The pass checklist records one attempt for each task, but Syifa's transcript includes instructions and a repeated recording exercise. Completion with support is the defensible result. ## Main interpretation

The shared board and personal paper worked as one study activity. Kai could look between them without leaving the desk. The guide helped Syifa recover during use. Keeping the paper fixed and giving the right controller a clear writing role supports the intended workflow. These findings are consistent with earlier design changes, but the study did not isolate each change or compare identical participant groups.

The original educational concept is partially confirmed. Users understood and completed the classroom activity, but the test did not measure improved concentration, retention, or learning. High satisfaction should not be treated as proof of educational benefit.

## Final reflection

The report reviews Syifa's IP2b session. Her positive ratings and successful task completion coexisted with guide use and moderator help. This shows why transcripts matter alongside scores.

Paper Wizard-of-Oz testing allowed rapid exploration of gesture meanings, while Unity testing exposed tool visibility and view problems. Physical-controller testing addressed the device interaction. Think aloud produced useful expectations, but leading prompts reduced the evidence for independent discovery. Three notes-only sessions and missing measurement logs also limit the conclusions.

## Design direction

Keep the comfortable viewpoints, fixed paper, separate controller roles, and accessible guide. Test a short practice activity and clearer recording states before adding more features. Note saving is a more direct study improvement than a larger room or multiplayer.

A future passthrough AR prototype could place a virtual board above a real desk. Compare it with VR using the same note-taking task and measure anchoring, readability, distraction, comfort, and note quality. AR remains a proposed extension rather than an implemented or evaluated feature.
